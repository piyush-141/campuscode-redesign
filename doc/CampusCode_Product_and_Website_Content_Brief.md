# CampusCode --- Product, Positioning & Website Content Brief

> **Purpose:** This document is the single source of truth for
> explaining CampusCode and shaping its public-facing landing page. It
> defines the product, audiences, value proposition, features,
> differentiators, messaging hierarchy, content strategy, and conversion
> principles.
>
> **Content rule:** Be specific about what CampusCode helps people do.
> Do not invent metrics, partnerships, customer logos, integrations,
> guarantees, or capabilities that have not been verified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Product at a glance

**Product name:** CampusCode\
**Category:** AI-powered hackathon platform\
**Core proposition:** One platform for students to discover and
participate in hackathons, and for organisers to create, manage,
evaluate, and conclude them.

**Short description**

CampusCode brings the hackathon lifecycle into one connected platform.
Students can discover events, register, find teammates, submit ideas and
projects, follow rounds and results, and access verifiable certificates.
Organisers can create hackathons, manage participants and submissions,
coordinate rounds, and use AI-assisted tools to support screening and
evaluation.

**Tagline:** Build. Compete. Ship.

**Primary message:** Run hackathons and participate in them without
coordinating the entire experience across disconnected tools.

### What CampusCode is

CampusCode is a shared platform for the people involved in a hackathon:

-   **Students and participants** looking for opportunities to build,
    compete, collaborate, and demonstrate their work.
-   **Organisers** who need a structured way to create events, manage
    registrations, run rounds, review submissions, and publish outcomes.
-   **Evaluators and reviewers** who need organised submissions and
    AI-assisted evaluation workflows.

### What CampusCode is not

Avoid describing CampusCode as:

-   Only a hackathon listing website.
-   Only a team-finding or networking app.
-   Only an AI judging tool.
-   A generic learning management system.
-   A guarantee of winning, selection, placement, or project success.
-   A fully autonomous judging system unless that capability is
    explicitly confirmed.

The platform connects event discovery and participation with event
operations. AI tools support selected parts of that journey; they are
not the entire product.

## 2. The problem CampusCode addresses

A hackathon involves more than announcing an event. The process can span
discovery, registration, participant approvals, team formation, idea
submission, project development, round-wise submissions, evaluation,
results, and certificates.

When these activities are handled through separate tools, information
and coordination become fragmented.

### Common friction for organisers

-   Registrations arrive through forms or separate channels.
-   Participant approvals and event records need manual coordination.
-   Team and submission information can be spread across different
    places.
-   Multiple rounds create additional tracking and administration.
-   Evaluation can become difficult to organise consistently.
-   Publishing results and distributing certificates can require
    repetitive work.

### Common friction for students

-   Finding relevant hackathons can be difficult.
-   Finding teammates with complementary skills takes effort.
-   Event rules and instructions may be difficult to navigate.
-   Submission requirements and round progress may be spread across
    channels.
-   Participants may lack one clear place to check results and
    recognition.

### The central problem statement

**Hackathons bring people together to build things. The tools used to
run them should not keep everyone busy coordinating the process.**

Use this problem statement to introduce the product. Do not repeat the
same list of scattered forms, chats, spreadsheets, and manual work in
every later section.

## 3. Positioning and unique selling propositions

### Primary USP

**A connected hackathon lifecycle for both organisers and students,
supported by purpose-built AI tools.**

This is the main differentiator to communicate. CampusCode should not be
positioned as a collection of unrelated features. Its value comes from
connecting the stages of a hackathon in one place.

### Supporting differentiators

1.  **Two-sided platform:** Organisers can run events while students
    discover and participate in them.
2.  **End-to-end event workflow:** Event creation, registration,
    approvals, rounds, submissions, evaluation, results, and
    certificates are part of the same product experience.
3.  **Purpose-built AI toolkit:** IdeaCheck, HackMate, RuleBot, round
    analysers, and the student-panel chatbot address specific hackathon
    tasks.
4.  **Team discovery:** HackMate supports finding teammates, including
    search using CampusCode ID.
5.  **Structured evaluation support:** R1, R2, and R3 analysers provide
    AI assistance for round-based review workflows.
6.  **Participant continuity:** Students can follow event progress,
    results, certificates, and their Digital ID from the platform.
7.  **Organiser control:** Organisers can manage registrations,
    approvals, rounds, submissions, and evaluation from a dedicated
    workflow.

### Messaging hierarchy

Use these points in this order:

1.  **What it does:** Run and join hackathons in one platform.
2.  **Why it matters:** Fewer disconnected workflows for organisers and
    participants.
3.  **What makes it different:** A connected lifecycle plus specialised
    AI tools.
4.  **Proof:** Show real product screens and verifiable product
    capabilities.
5.  **Action:** Explore hackathons or run an event.

Do not lead with a long list of AI model names or technical
implementation details. Visitors first need to understand the product
and its practical value.

## 7. Conversion-focused content rules

The landing page must not feel like a long feature inventory. It should
help visitors understand the product, recognise their own use case, see
evidence, and take a next step.

### Rule 1: One section, one job

Every section must have a distinct purpose:

  Section                 Its unique job
  ----------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  Hero                    Explain the product and offer the two main paths.
  Audience introduction   Help visitors identify their role.
  Problem                 Make the current friction recognisable.
  Platform overview       Explain the connected lifecycle.
  AI toolkit              Demonstrate what the AI tools specifically do.
  How it works            Explain the sequence of using CampusCode.
  Student section         Convert students.
  Organiser section       Convert organisers.
  Product proof           Establish credibility through real interfaces.
  FAQ                     Resolve objections and uncertainty.
  Final CTA               Prompt the next action.

If two sections answer the same question, merge them or change the
purpose of one.

### Rule 2: Do not repeat generic phrases

Avoid repeatedly using: - "Everything in one place." - "Seamless
experience." - "Powered by AI." - "Simplify your workflow." - "Take your
hackathon to the next level." - "All-in-one platform." - "From idea to
execution."

These phrases can appear once when useful, but should not become the
language of every section.

Replace vague benefits with concrete actions: - Instead of "Seamless
team collaboration," say "Find potential teammates through HackMate." -
Instead of "Smart event management," say "Create events, approve
registrations, and manage rounds." - Instead of "AI-powered evaluation,"
explain what the R1, R2, and R3 analysers support. - Instead of
"Complete recognition," say "Access verifiable certificates and a
QR-based Digital ID."

### Rule 3: Write headings that communicate value

A heading should tell visitors something useful, not merely label a
feature.

Weak: "Our Features"\
Better: "Everything an organiser needs to run each round."

Weak: "AI Tools"\
Better: "Purpose-built AI tools for the work around a hackathon."

Weak: "For Students"\
Better: "Find opportunities, teammates, and a place to show your work."

Weak: "Platform"\
Better: "One connected workflow from event creation to results."

### Rule 4: Keep supporting copy concise

Use this structure: 1. A specific headline. 2. One or two sentences
explaining the value. 3. A clear visual demonstration. 4. One relevant
action where appropriate.

Do not put the full feature list into every section. Use detail where it
helps the visitor make a decision.

### Rule 5: Make every visual prove something

Every product image should answer a question: - Can I discover events? -
Can an organiser manage registrations? - How do I find teammates? - What
does IdeaCheck show? - How are rounds and submissions managed? - What
does the evaluation interface look like? - What does a certificate or
Digital ID look like?

Avoid decorative screenshots with unreadable text, generic AI-generated
dashboards, or repeated use of the same interface.

### Rule 6: Use audience-specific CTAs

Use: - **Explore Hackathons** for students. - **Run a Hackathon** for
organisers. - **See How It Works** for visitors who need explanation. -
**View Product** for visitors who want proof.

Do not use "Get Started" as the only CTA everywhere. It is less
informative than an action that identifies the visitor's intent.

### Rule 7: Build trust honestly

Use: - Real product screenshots. - Actual event examples, where
permitted. - Genuine organiser or participant testimonials. - Verified
partner or college logos. - Accurate feature descriptions. - Real usage
statistics only when measured and approved.

Never fabricate: - Number of students or organisers. - Number of
hackathons hosted. - College partnerships. - AI accuracy or judging
performance. - Testimonials or ratings. - Certificates' external
acceptance.

If proof is not available, demonstrate the product instead of
manufacturing social proof.

## 8. Visual and interaction guidance

### Design direction

Use a modern, clean, product-led SaaS design inspired by the supplied
Aceternity reference, while retaining CampusCode's own visual identity.

**Brand palette** - Primary purple: `#7665FF` - Lime accent: `#A8E62E` -
Background: `#F7F7F4` - Primary text: `#111111` - Cards: White with
subtle neutral borders.

**Typography** - Inter for primary interface and marketing copy. - DM
Mono for small labels, metadata, section identifiers, and technical
accents.

**Layout** - Generous whitespace. - Clear heading hierarchy. -
Alternating split layouts for feature explanations. - Large product
previews where they provide evidence. - Consistent card radii and
restrained shadows. - Responsive layouts that prioritise readability
over preserving desktop compositions.

### Animation principles

Use motion to support hierarchy and make the product feel responsive.

-   Hero: headline, description, CTAs, and product preview enter in a
    short sequence.
-   Section headings: subtle fade, upward movement, and blur-to-sharp
    reveal.
-   Feature cards: staggered entrance.
-   Product screenshots: gentle scale and opacity reveal.
-   Buttons: clear hover and pressed states.
-   Cards: restrained lift and border transition.
-   Navigation: smooth anchor scrolling.

Animations should be short, consistent, and performant. Respect
reduced-motion preferences. Avoid animating every element or making
content invisible until a scroll animation runs.

### Product screenshot policy

Prefer actual CampusCode screenshots. If a screen is not ready: 1. Use a
clearly labelled conceptual mockup only if necessary. 2. Do not present
a conceptual screen as an existing feature. 3. Do not add fake metrics,
names, event records, or partner logos. 4. Replace temporary mockups
with real product screenshots before launch.

## 11. Guidance for the AI or developer writing the website

Treat this document as the product and messaging reference when creating
or revising CampusCode's landing page.

The implementation should:

1.  Preserve the distinction between student and organiser journeys.
2.  Explain the complete hackathon lifecycle without turning the page
    into a feature dump.
3.  Give each section one unique communication objective.
4.  Use the exact feature names: IdeaCheck, HackMate, RuleBot, R1/R2/R3
    analysers, and student-panel chatbot.
5.  Explain features through practical outcomes rather than generic AI
    marketing language.
6.  Use real product interfaces as visual evidence wherever possible.
7.  Keep the copy concise, specific, and conversion-oriented.
8.  Avoid repeated headlines, repeated descriptions, and repeated visual
    compositions.
9.  Never invent capabilities, metrics, testimonials, integrations, or
    proof.
10. Make the next action obvious for both students and organisers.
11. Keep CampusCode's purple, lime, and neutral design identity
    consistent.
12. Ensure all copy remains accurate to the current product
    implementation.

### Final quality test

Before accepting any section, ask:

-   Does this section communicate a new piece of information?
-   Does it answer a real visitor question?
-   Is the benefit concrete and supported by an actual capability?
-   Is the visual demonstrating something specific?
-   Is the wording different from the sections around it?
-   Does the section help a student or organiser move closer to an
    action?

If the answer to the first two questions is no, remove or rewrite the
section.

that connects the people, operations, submissions, evaluation, and
outcomes involved in running and participating in hackathons.

**Build. Compete. Ship.**

That is the brand promise. Every section of the website should make one
part of that promise tangible.
